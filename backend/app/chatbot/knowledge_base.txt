KB-01 — What is NestScore?

NestScore is a free, anonymous student housing rating platform dedicated
to students at Meru University of Science and Technology (MUST) in Kenya.
 
The platform exists because finding honest information about student
housing near MUST is genuinely hard. Most students rely on word of mouth,
which is unreliable and biased. NestScore gives every student — past and
present — a place to share their real experience anonymously, so the next
student can make a more informed decision before signing a lease.
 
Tagline: 'Know your plot before you sign.'
 
NestScore is operated by NestScore Community — an anonymous group of
MUST students committed to housing transparency. The operators choose to
remain anonymous to protect themselves from landlord pressure and to
ensure the platform's neutrality is never compromised by personal
relationships or interests.
 
NestScore is not affiliated with MUST, with any landlord, or with any
housing agency. It is an independent, student-driven platform.
 
NestScore is free to use. There are no accounts, no sign-ups, and no
personal information required from students.

KB-02 — Mission and Purpose

NestScore's mission is to give every MUST student the honest housing
information they deserve before committing to a rental agreement.
 
Three core goals:
 
1. TRANSPARENCY — Surface honest, peer-sourced insights about plots
   near MUST so students know what they are getting into before they pay
   a deposit.
 
2. ACCOUNTABILITY — Create a constructive public feedback loop that
   motivates landlords to improve living standards. A landlord who knows
   their property is being rated has an incentive to maintain it.
 
3. SAFETY — Help students avoid unsafe, overpriced, or misrepresented
   housing. A student who avoids a bad plot because of a NestScore
   review is exactly the outcome the platform exists to enable.
 
NestScore is not anti-landlord. Good landlords benefit from the platform
because their quality shows up in ratings and students actively seek them
out. The platform creates a genuine market incentive to improve standards.

KB-03 — Anonymity and Student Privacy

NestScore is built privacy-first. Here is exactly what the platform
does and does not collect:
 
WHAT IS NEVER COLLECTED:
- Your name
- Your student ID or registration number
- Your email address
- Your phone number
- Your exact location
- Any document or identifying file
 
HOW ANONYMITY IS PROTECTED:
NestScore uses technical measures to prevent duplicate or fake reviews
without identifying who you are. These measures work in the background
and do not store any personal information about you. The system can
tell that a device has already reviewed a plot, but it cannot tell whose
device it is or who is behind it.
 
YOUR REVIEW IS YOUR OWN:
Once submitted, your review is displayed anonymously. There is no
account attached to it. No one — including the NestScore team — can
trace a review back to a specific student.
 
DATA PROTECTION:
NestScore operates in compliance with the Kenya Data Protection Act 2019.
Because no personal data is collected, there is very little to protect —
which is the point. The safest data is data that was never collected.

KB-04 — How Reviews Work

SUBMITTING A REVIEW:
Any student can submit a review for any plot listed on NestScore.
You do not need an account. The process is:
  1. Find the plot on the map or browse list
  2. Open the plot's page
  3. Select a star rating from 1 to 5
  4. Write your review (minimum 80 characters, maximum 2000 characters)
  5. Complete the security check
  6. Submit
 
WHAT HAPPENS AFTER YOU SUBMIT:
Your review goes through a brief quality check before appearing publicly.
This is to prevent spam and ensure the platform stays useful. Most
legitimate reviews appear within a short period of submission.
 
ONE REVIEW PER PLOT:
You can only submit one review per plot. This is a platform integrity
rule — it prevents the same person from flooding a plot with multiple
ratings in either direction.
 
REVIEWS ARE PERMANENT:
Because NestScore has no accounts, there is no way to identify the
author of a review after submission. This means reviews cannot be
edited or deleted by the student who wrote them. Think carefully
before you submit — make it count.
 
WHAT MAKES A GOOD REVIEW:
The most useful reviews are specific and honest. Tell future students
what they actually need to know. Good things to include:
  - Water availability and reliability
  - Electricity and power outage frequency
  - Security — gates, lighting, incidents
  - Caretaker responsiveness
  - Distance from MUST main gate
  - Noise levels and neighbourhood feel
  - Pest issues (cockroaches, rats, bedbugs)
  - Value for money relative to the rent charged
  - Anything you wish you had known before moving in
 
WHAT NOT TO INCLUDE:
  - The full name of any individual (landlord, caretaker, neighbour)
  - Unverifiable factual claims stated as fact
  - Offensive, hateful, or discriminatory language
  - Anything unrelated to the housing experience
  - Commercial promotions or advertising

KB-05 — The Rating Score Explained

Each plot on NestScore has a rating score displayed prominently on its
page and on the map pin. Here is how to read it:
 
STAR SCALE (1-5):
  5 stars — Excellent. Students highly recommend this plot.
  4 stars — Good. Minor issues but generally a solid choice.
  3 stars — Mixed. Has both good and bad qualities. Read the reviews.
  2 stars — Poor. Significant issues. Proceed with caution.
  1 star  — Very poor. Students strongly advise against this plot.
 
THE NESTSCORE WEIGHTED RATING:
The score shown is not a simple average. NestScore uses a weighted
system that accounts for the number of reviews a plot has received.
This prevents a plot with only 2 or 3 reviews from appearing perfectly
rated when there is not enough data to be confident.
 
In plain terms: a plot needs a meaningful number of honest reviews
before its score stabilises and reflects the true picture. A new plot
with very few reviews will have its score adjusted toward the platform
average until more data comes in.
 
WHAT THE MAP COLOURS MEAN:
  Green pin  — Weighted score 4.0 and above. Good to go.
  Amber pin  — Weighted score 3.0 to 3.9. Mixed — read reviews.
  Red pin    — Weighted score below 3.0. Exercise caution.
  Grey pin   — No ratings yet. Be the first to review.
  Pulsing pin — Score temporarily frozen. Under review for anomalies.
 
RATING BREAKDOWN CHART:
Each plot page shows a bar chart of how ratings are distributed across
all five star levels. This tells you more than the average alone.
A plot with mostly 5-star and 1-star ratings (bimodal distribution)
tells a very different story from a plot where everyone gave 3 stars.

KB-06 — Plot Listings and the Map

WHAT IS A PLOT:
On NestScore, 'plot' refers to any student rental property — bedsitters,
single rooms, double rooms, hostels, and apartments — near MUST.
The term is used in its common Kenyan student housing context.
 
AREAS COVERED:
NestScore currently covers the main student housing zones near MUST:
  - Nchiru — the primary student housing corridor. Highest density of
    bedsitters and single rooms. Most students live here.
  - Katheri — secondary zone. Mix of hostels and private plots.
    Slightly further from the MUST main gate.
  - Campus-adjacent — plots within approximately 500 metres of the
    MUST main gate. Higher rent but shortest walk to class.
  - Kianjai, Mascan, Kunene — other nearby areas with student housing.
 
THE INTERACTIVE MAP:
The NestScore homepage shows an interactive map centred on MUST campus.
Every listed plot appears as a colour-coded pin on the map.
Click any pin to see the plot name, its rating, and a link to the full
plot page with all reviews.
 
BROWSING PLOTS:
Below the map is a list of all plots sorted by weighted score.
You can filter by area and by minimum star rating.
The search bar filters both the map and the list simultaneously.
 
WHY A PLOT IS LISTED:
NestScore lists all known student plots near MUST regardless of whether
the landlord wants to be listed. If students live there, it is listable.
This is the same policy used by platforms like TripAdvisor and Google
Reviews — a business cannot opt out of being reviewed.
 
SUGGESTING A NEW PLOT:
If you know of a student plot near MUST that is not yet on NestScore,
you can suggest it using the suggestion form. The NestScore team reviews
all suggestions before a plot goes live.

KB-07 — Review Authenticity and Anti-Fraud

NestScore takes review integrity seriously. The platform has multiple
layers of protection against fake, manipulated, or spam reviews.
The specific technical details of these protections are not disclosed —
revealing them would help bad actors work around them.
 
WHAT THE PLATFORM PROTECTS AGAINST:
  - A landlord submitting fake 5-star reviews for their own property
  - A competitor submitting fake 1-star reviews to damage a rival
  - Bots submitting mass reviews automatically
  - Coordinated groups artificially inflating or deflating ratings
  - The same person submitting multiple reviews for the same plot
 
HOW YOU CAN TELL IF SOMETHING LOOKS WRONG:
If a plot's score appears suspicious, NestScore may display an
'Under Review' badge. This means the platform has detected unusual
rating activity and the score has been temporarily frozen pending
investigation. The badge is transparent — students can see it and
know to treat the current score with caution.
 
WHY THE RATING SYSTEM IS TRUSTWORTHY:
NestScore uses a weighted scoring formula that reduces the impact of
plots with very few ratings. A landlord who gets 3 friends to submit
5-star reviews will not see their plot jump to a perfect score.
The system is designed to reward consistent, genuine, high-volume
positive feedback — not gaming.
 
PROFANITY AND QUALITY FILTERING:
All reviews pass through content filters before publication.
Low-quality, spammy, or harmful content is screened out automatically.
This keeps the platform useful and legally compliant.

KB-08 — Landlord Relations and the Dispute Process

NESTSCORE IS FAIR TO LANDLORDS:
The platform is not anti-landlord. It is pro-student-information.
Good landlords benefit directly from NestScore — their quality shows
up in ratings and students actively seek out well-rated plots.
The platform creates a genuine market incentive to improve standards.
 
LANDLORDS CANNOT OPT OUT:
A landlord cannot request that their property be removed from NestScore.
This is a core policy. If students live there, it is listable.
This is how all major review platforms work.
 
WHAT LANDLORDS CAN DO:
Landlords who want to engage with the platform can:
  1. CLAIM their plot — verify ownership and get a 'Landlord Active'
     badge that shows students the landlord is engaged
  2. FILE A DISPUTE on a specific review they believe is unfair or
     inaccurate — their response is shown publicly next to the review
     so students can see both sides
 
HOW THE DISPUTE PROCESS WORKS:
  Step 1 — Landlord claims the plot by verifying ownership
  Step 2 — Landlord submits a dispute form for a specific review
  Step 3 — A 'Dispute Filed' badge appears on that review
  Step 4 — The landlord's response is shown alongside the review
  Step 5 — NestScore reviews the dispute and either dismisses it or
            takes action, with the outcome shown publicly
 
KEY PRINCIPLE:
Neither the review nor the landlord's response is removed by default.
Both sides are shown publicly. Transparency is preserved.
Students can read the review AND the landlord's response and decide
for themselves what to believe.

KB-09 — Reporting and Flagging Reviews

FLAGGING A REVIEW:
If you see a review that you believe violates NestScore's community
guidelines, you can flag it using the flag button on that review.
Flagging is anonymous.
 
WHAT HAPPENS WHEN YOU FLAG:
Flagged reviews are reviewed by the NestScore moderation system.
If multiple students flag the same review, it may be temporarily
hidden while being assessed. A badge is shown to make this visible.
 
VALID REASONS TO FLAG:
  - The review contains someone's full name
  - The review contains hate speech or discriminatory content
  - The review appears to be spam or advertising
  - The review contains false factual claims stated as definite fact
  - The review is clearly not about the housing experience
 
WHAT FLAGGING IS NOT FOR:
  - You disagree with the opinion expressed in the review
  - The review is negative about your property or a property you like
  - You want to silence a review you find inconvenient
 
Flagging is for genuine policy violations, not for suppressing
legitimate student opinions, however negative they may be.

KB-10 — Legal Status and Compliance

ARE REVIEWS LEGAL IN KENYA?
Yes. Sharing honest opinions about products and services — including
rental housing — is protected speech under Kenya's Constitution,
specifically Article 33 (Freedom of Expression) and Article 34
(Freedom of the Media and Freedom to Publish).
 
Anonymous publishing is also legally protected. NestScore's operators
choosing to remain anonymous is a legal and internationally recognised
practice for accountability platforms.
 
WHAT KEEPS REVIEWS LEGAL:
NestScore reviews are legal because they are opinions and personal
experiences, not unverifiable factual claims about named individuals.
The platform enforces this through content rules:
  - Reviews must describe the property experience, not individuals
  - No full names of landlords, caretakers, or other individuals
  - No defamatory factual claims stated as definitive fact
  - Opinions and personal experiences are protected speech
 
DATA PROTECTION:
NestScore operates in compliance with the Kenya Data Protection Act 2019.
No personal data as defined by the Act is collected from students.
 
PLATFORM DISCLAIMER:
NestScore hosts user-submitted opinions about rental properties.
We do not verify individual claims. Content represents the personal
views of anonymous student users, not NestScore Community or MUST.
Property managers may formally dispute reviews via our dispute process.
 
IF A LANDLORD THREATENS LEGAL ACTION:
NestScore cannot provide legal advice to individual students.
If you are personally threatened or harassed by a landlord in relation
to a review, please consult a legal professional or contact the
relevant Kenyan authorities.

KB-11 — Community Guidelines (Full)

WHAT MAKES A GREAT NESTSCORE REVIEW:
 
DO include:
  - Your honest overall experience living at or visiting the plot
  - Specific details about water, electricity, security, cleanliness
  - Distance and transport information (how far from MUST main gate,
    available transport options, road condition in rainy season)
  - Value for money — is the rent fair for what you get?
  - What surprised you — good or bad — that you did not expect
  - What you wish you had known before moving in
  - Whether you would recommend this plot to a fellow student and why
 
DO NOT include:
  - The full name of any individual (landlord, caretaker, other tenants)
  - Unverifiable factual accusations (e.g. 'the landlord is a thief'
    — instead say 'I had issues with my deposit not being returned')
  - Offensive, hateful, or discriminatory language of any kind
  - Personal attacks on individuals
  - Content completely unrelated to the housing experience
  - Promotional content or advertising
 
LENGTH GUIDELINES:
  Minimum: 80 characters — enough to say something meaningful
  Maximum: 2000 characters — enough to be thorough without rambling
 
EXAMPLE OF A GOOD REVIEW:
'Stayed here for two semesters. Water is available most days but
cuts out for 2-3 days at a time every few weeks. Electricity has
been reliable. Security is decent — there is a gate and a caretaker
who lives on site. About a 15 minute walk to MUST main gate.
Rent is 3500 per month for a bedsitter which is fair for the area.
Main issue is the walls are thin so you hear everything from the
next room. Overall not bad for the price.'
 
EXAMPLE OF A REVIEW THAT WILL BE REJECTED:
'John Ochieng the landlord is a scammer and a terrible person.'
(Names an individual and makes an accusation, not a housing experience.)

KB-12 — Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Can I review a plot I visited but did not live in?
A: Yes. If you have direct, firsthand knowledge of the property —
   from visiting, staying briefly, or helping a friend move in —
   your experience is valid and useful.
 
Q: What if the plot I want to review is not listed?
A: Use the 'Suggest a Plot' form. The NestScore team reviews all
   suggestions and adds verified plots to the platform.
 
Q: Can I change or delete my review?
A: No. Because NestScore has no accounts, there is no way to verify
   that a person requesting a change is the original author.
   Reviews are permanent once published. Write carefully.
 
Q: Can my landlord find out I wrote a review?
A: No. NestScore does not collect your identity and displays no
   identifying information on reviews. There is nothing to find.
 
Q: Can a landlord remove a review they do not like?
A: No. Landlords can dispute a review and have their response shown
   publicly alongside it — but they cannot remove it.
 
Q: My landlord threatened me. What do I do?
A: NestScore cannot provide legal advice. If you feel threatened,
   contact the relevant Kenyan authorities. The review you submitted
   is protected speech and you submitted it anonymously.
 
Q: How do I contact NestScore?
A: Use the contact form on the platform. No email address is displayed
   publicly. The team reads all contact form submissions.
 
Q: Is NestScore affiliated with MUST?
A: No. NestScore is completely independent. It is not run by or
   affiliated with MUST, any student union, or any housing agency.
 
Q: Is NestScore free?
A: Yes. NestScore is and will always be free for students.
 
Q: Why is a plot showing 'Under Review'?
A: The platform detected unusual rating activity on that plot —
   such as many reviews appearing very quickly, or ratings that
   look coordinated. The score is temporarily frozen while the
   situation is investigated. Treat that score with caution.
 
Q: Can I review in Swahili or Sheng?
A: Yes. Write in whatever language feels natural to you.
   NestScore's content filters understand common Kenyan terms.
